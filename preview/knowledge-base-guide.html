What CuratorOS is now

CuratorOS is the structured knowledge layer underneath Ocean Liner Curator. It takes information already present across OceanLiners.net and related project data, turns that information into standardized records and relationships, tracks where the knowledge came from, helps identify gaps and useful patterns, and recombines reviewed knowledge into new research collections and publication drafts.

Core idea: webpages are representations of knowledge. CuratorOS stores and connects the underlying knowledge so it can be checked, reused, combined, and published in more than one form.

1. The CuratorOS knowledge pipeline

OceanLiners.net + project files
Existing ship guides, essays, hubs, reference objects, photographs, indexes, source lists, and machine-readable project data provide raw material.
site-index.json
Acts as the website manifest: the durable table of contents CuratorOS uses to understand what exists publicly and what may need extraction or review.
Extraction
CuratorOS reads saved webpage HTML and identifies candidate facts, narrative material, metadata, and entity references.
Project Records
Approved information becomes normalized records for ships, companies, organizations, people, objects, sources, photographs, and other entities.
Entity Resolution
Duplicate or variant identities are consolidated around stable canonical records while alternate names are preserved as aliases.
Knowledge Graph
Relationships connect records: built by, operated by, associated with, depicts, same as, and other explicit links.
Knowledge Intelligence
CuratorOS derives useful clusters and signals from the connected corpus, such as builder fleets, operator fleets, classes, routes, hubs, and relationship gaps.
Knowledge Explorer
Records can be queried and saved as reusable knowledge collections.
Publication Composer
Collections can be shaped into structured briefs and reviewable HTML drafts for future OceanLiners.net pages.

The system is deliberately review-first. CuratorOS may suggest, normalize, compare, and assemble, but it does not silently rewrite the live site.

2. The basic mental model

A Project Record represents an identifiable thing, not a webpage. RMS Olympic is a ship record. Harland & Wolff is a company record. A menu is an object record. A source can support many records. A webpage may expose one record, several records, or only part of a record.

Do not duplicate derived knowledge unnecessarily. If a builder fleet can be obtained by querying every ship with a built_by relationship to one builder record, CuratorOS should derive the fleet rather than maintain a second hard-coded list.

4. The role of site-index.json

site-index.json is not the knowledge base. It is the website manifest. OceanLiners.net remains authoritative about which public pages exist; CuratorOS remains authoritative about what has been extracted, standardized, reviewed, connected, and reused.

Loading a newer site index allows CuratorOS to compare the current site against its prior manifest and the knowledge corpus. When modification timestamps, hashes, or checksums are available, they can help identify pages that may require re-extraction.

The goal is incremental maintenance: process only what is new, changed, unmatched, or not yet understood instead of repeatedly rebuilding the entire corpus.

5. Project Records and canonical identity

Each record has a stable ID, type, title, publication/review status, structured data, relationships, sources, notes, metadata, and provenance as applicable. Stable IDs matter because relationships and saved collections should point to identities rather than fragile display text.

Common record types include ships, companies, organizations, people, sources, objects, photographs, and media. A record may be created through import, extraction, or deliberate local editing.

When different records represent the same real entity, use Entity Resolution. The selected canonical record remains active; alternate records are archived and linked to it rather than silently erased.

6. Evidence, provenance, and uncertainty

CuratorOS should distinguish the standardized value from the evidence that produced it. Extraction preserves the source webpage and raw wording so normalization does not erase provenance.

  • Known: adequately supported and reviewed.
  • Probable: supported but still dependent on interpretation or incomplete evidence.
  • Uncertain: disputed, incomplete, approximate, or awaiting stronger evidence.

Conflicting source values should be surfaced for review instead of collapsed into false certainty. “Unknown” is an acceptable result.

Direction of travel: CuratorOS should increasingly preserve fact-level evidence—raw value, normalized value, source, locator, confidence, and review state—rather than only page-level provenance.

7. From connected knowledge to reusable collections

Knowledge Explorer collections are reusable views over stable record IDs. They can originate from a manual query or from Knowledge Intelligence.

Examples include ships built by Harland & Wolff, ships operated by White Star Line, vessels sharing a class, route-centered groups, or any custom combination supported by standardized fields and relationships.

A collection should remain linked to the underlying records instead of becoming a detached copy. That allows future record improvements to flow into later research and publication work.

8. Publication is a view over knowledge

Publication Composer is intentionally separated from the knowledge store. It selects records, proposes a page pattern, creates a section plan, and generates reviewable HTML material without pretending that the draft is already a final OceanLiners.net page.

Supported planning patterns include fleet directories, builder collections, timelines, comparisons, research tables, illustrated articles, hubs, and custom briefs.

The eventual publication layer should map these briefs into the site’s actual house templates and publish through a reviewable GitHub branch and pull request rather than writing directly to the live site.

9. Safety and change boundaries

  • Extraction does not modify the source HTML.
  • Project Record edits become local pending changes first.
  • Entity consolidation archives duplicates instead of deleting them silently.
  • Knowledge Graph and Knowledge Intelligence analysis are read-only unless you explicitly save a derived collection.
  • Publication Composer produces drafts and packages; it does not publish to OceanLiners.net.
  • Quick Backup should be used before substantial local work.
  • Source-file updates and future publishing should remain reviewable through diffs and GitHub pull requests.

10. Recommended working rhythm

  1. Generate the latest site-index.json when the website has materially changed.
  2. Load it in Site / Knowledge Sync and review new, changed, unmatched, and unextracted pages.
  3. Use Extract Knowledge for one page or Build Corpus for a batch of saved HTML pages.
  4. Review and approve normalized knowledge into Project Records.
  5. Resolve duplicate entities and unresolved relationship targets.
  6. Explore the graph and review Knowledge Intelligence opportunities.
  7. Save useful groups as Knowledge Explorer collections.
  8. Use Publication Composer when a collection has a clear editorial purpose.
  9. Export or back up important local work before clearing browser data or changing devices.

11. Frequently asked questions

Is CuratorOS the website CMS?

No. It is the knowledge and research layer underneath the site. Its job is to understand, normalize, connect, audit, and recombine project knowledge. Page assembly and publishing are downstream actions.

Is site-index.json the database?

No. It is the public-site manifest—the inventory of pages. Project Records are the normalized knowledge corpus.

Does every webpage become exactly one Project Record?

No. A page can supply knowledge to one record, several records, or no new entity record at all. The page is evidence/provenance; the record represents the underlying entity or item.

Can CuratorOS change OceanLiners.net automatically?

Not in the current architecture. The system deliberately produces local changes, reports, extraction reviews, collections, and publication drafts first. Future source publishing should be performed through explicit GitHub branches, diffs, and pull requests.

Why use relationships instead of copying names into every record?

Relationships give one real entity one canonical identity. They also let CuratorOS derive fleets, builder groups, reverse links, graph paths, and publication collections without maintaining duplicate lists.

What happens when two sources disagree?

The disagreement should remain visible for review. CuratorOS should not manufacture certainty simply because a structured field expects one value.

What is Knowledge Intelligence?

It is the derived-analysis layer. It notices meaningful groups already implicit in the corpus—such as several ships sharing a builder, operator, class, route, or country—and offers them as research or publication opportunities.

What happened to Full Workspaces?

The legacy launcher was retired after the native workflow replaced its practical functions. The old source modules remain in the repository as historical/reference code, but they are no longer part of the active CuratorOS interface.

Where is CuratorOS headed next?

Toward stronger fact-level provenance, richer relationship intelligence, template-aware page assembly, and a safe GitHub publishing engine that turns reviewed knowledge into reviewable site changes.
