Running the CuratorOS Knowledge Base

CuratorOS is not simply a list of ships or a place to store finished prose. Its knowledge base is the structured editorial memory of Ocean Liner Curator: records, evidence, relationships, review status, media, notes, and publication-ready data kept in a form that can be searched, checked, reused, and improved over time.

1. The basic mental model

Think of the knowledge base as a collection of individual records. Each record represents one identifiable thing: a ship, shipping line, builder, source, reference object, photograph, or another supported entity. A record should describe that thing once, then connect it to other records rather than repeating the same information everywhere.

Core rule: create one authoritative record for each entity, then connect records through relationships and sources.

2. Choosing the correct record type

Ship

Use for an individual vessel. Typical fields include builder, operator, yard number, launch date, maiden voyage, dimensions, tonnage, speed, and service details. Do not create a second ship record merely because the vessel changed names; preserve the vessel as one entity unless CuratorOS specifically requires separate identities.

Builder

Use for a shipyard or shipbuilding company. Store the builder’s own facts here, then link ships to it with a built by relationship.

Shipping line or company

Use for an operating company, line, or corporate entity. Link ships through operated by, owned by, or another accurate relationship. Avoid putting a company biography into every ship record.

Source

Use for books, archival records, plans, registers, articles, official documents, or other evidence. A source record should be identifiable and citable. It may support many other records.

Reference object

Use for physical collection material such as menus, brochures, passenger lists, keys, stationery, or deck plans. Include dimensions, material, condition, storage location, date, and associated ship or company where known.

Photograph or media

Use for cataloged visual material. Record creator, date, depicted subject, source, caption, alt text, rights, and attribution. Distinguish what the image depicts from where the image came from.

3. Recommended daily workflow

  1. Make a Quick Backup. Do this before a substantial editing session or import.
  2. Open Findings. Review missing sources, incomplete metadata, review-state records, and imported scanner findings.
  3. Choose one bounded task. Examples: finish one ship record, attach sources to five records, or review one builder cluster.
  4. Open Full Workspaces. Use the specialized authoring and review tools for actual knowledge-base editing.
  5. Search before creating. Confirm that the entity does not already exist under another title or ID.
  6. Edit the record and its relationships. Add structured facts, supporting sources, confidence, and notes.
  7. Run the review tools. Resolve obvious gaps and inspect the publication preview.
  8. Change status only when justified. Draft means incomplete; review means substantially assembled but awaiting verification; published means suitable for trusted reuse.
  9. Export or back up again. End major sessions with a recoverable copy.

4. Adding a new knowledge record

Step 1: search for an existing record

Use Knowledge Records or Advanced Search. Search the exact name, alternate spellings, former names, abbreviations, and likely associated entities. Duplicate records fragment evidence and create conflicting facts.

Step 2: choose a stable ID

Use a predictable lowercase namespace. Examples: ship.aquitania, company.cunard-line, source.lloyds-register-1914, or object.olympic-menu-1929. Use hyphens inside the descriptive portion. Avoid dates or labels likely to change unless they distinguish the entity.

Step 3: enter the minimum identity fields

At minimum, provide ID, type, title, status, and a concise summary. Begin as draft when the record is skeletal or uncertain.

Step 4: add structured facts

Put facts into the appropriate fields instead of burying them in notes. Preserve original precision: do not convert an approximate date into a false exact date, and do not silently normalize conflicting measurements.

Step 5: attach sources

Add direct evidence whenever possible. A source should support the specific fact or relationship being asserted. A general website about a ship is weaker than a builder record, register, contemporary timetable, archival plan, or carefully documented secondary work.

Step 6: create relationships

Connect the record to existing entities. For a ship this often includes builder, operator, class, sister ships, source records, photographs, and associated objects. Add confidence and a note when the nature of the connection is not self-evident.

Step 7: review and preview

Use the Review Dashboard, Findings, Relationship Explorer, and Publication Preview. Verify that the record makes sense both alone and as part of the wider graph.

5. Sources, confidence, and uncertainty

CuratorOS should preserve the difference between evidence, interpretation, and uncertainty. A polished record is not necessarily a verified record.

  • Verified: directly supported by reliable evidence and checked.
  • Probable: well supported but not fully proven or dependent on secondary synthesis.
  • Possible or uncertain: plausible, disputed, incomplete, or awaiting stronger evidence.

When sources disagree, do not force a false consensus. Record the conflict in a curatorial note, preserve the competing values where the schema permits, and explain why one value is preferred. “Unknown” is a valid result.

Evidence-first practice: the database should reveal uncertainty rather than conceal it.

6. Building useful relationships

Relationships turn a catalog into a knowledge graph. They allow one source, company, builder, photograph, or object to be reused across many records without duplication.

  • Use the most precise relationship available.
  • Link to record IDs, not plain-text names, whenever the interface permits.
  • Include source IDs for relationships that make a historical claim.
  • Add a note when the relationship has qualifications or changed over time.
  • Inspect both directions in Relationship Explorer to catch isolated or mislinked records.

Example: RMS Olympic may be linked to Harland and Wolff as built by, White Star Line as operated by, a menu as associated with, a photograph as depicted by, and builder records as supporting evidence.

7. Review and publication lifecycle

Draft

The record exists but is incomplete, exploratory, or not sufficiently sourced. Draft records are working material.

Review

The record is substantially assembled and ready for deliberate verification. Use the Review Dashboard to check completeness, evidence, relationships, naming, and confidence.

Published

The record is suitable for trusted reuse in previews, exports, page packages, or public-facing content. Published does not mean permanently finished; it means the record currently meets the project’s standard.

Before publishing

  • Confirm the entity is not a duplicate.
  • Resolve high-priority Findings or document why they remain.
  • Check required type-specific fields.
  • Verify sources and relationship targets.
  • Review rights and attribution for media.
  • Inspect Publication Preview and Page Package Preview.
  • Set or update confidence and reviewed date.

9. Findings and imported scans

Findings are actionable warnings, not records. They point to work that may be needed: missing sources, missing metadata, review status, broken links, redirects, or opportunities found by another tool.

  • Open means the issue still needs attention.
  • Handled means you have resolved it or consciously accepted it.
  • Import scan adds findings from supported JSON or CSV reports.
  • Marking a finding handled does not itself change the underlying record or webpage.

After fixing the underlying issue, return to Findings and mark it handled. Imported findings remain locally stored until cleared or replaced.

10. Backups, imports, and safe operation

  • Use Quick Backup before imports, large edits, or experimental work.
  • Keep dated exports outside the browser. Browser storage should not be treated as the only copy.
  • Create a local snapshot before replacing the database.
  • Inspect imported files before confirming replacement.
  • Do not edit IDs casually after relationships have been created.
  • Use one primary working device at a time unless a sync workflow is explicitly active and tested.
Important: CuratorOS currently keeps core working state locally in the browser unless you deliberately export, import, ingest, or sync it. Clearing browser data may remove local changes that were never exported.

11. Periodic maintenance

Once a week or after a major content expansion:

  1. Export a complete backup.
  2. Review all high-priority Findings.
  3. Look for duplicate IDs, near-duplicate titles, and orphaned records.
  4. Inspect records still in draft or review.
  5. Check source records that are used broadly.
  6. Review media rights and attribution.
  7. Use Coverage Gap Intelligence and Relationship Explorer.
  8. Test a publication preview from several record types.

12. Troubleshooting

A record does not appear

Clear filters, search by ID, confirm that the intended catalog is loaded, and check whether the record exists only in another browser or backup.

A relationship target is missing

Create or import the target record first, verify its exact ID, then repair the relationship.

Findings return after an import

The imported catalog may still contain the underlying issue, or the finding ID may differ from the previously handled version. Fix the source data and reimport.

Data disappeared

Stop editing, avoid overwriting browser storage, and restore the most recent export or snapshot. Determine whether browser data was cleared or a different device/browser was used.

The Full Workspaces interface behaves oddly on iPad

Return to the rebuilt interface, make a backup, reload the page, and reopen Full Workspaces. The rebuilt shell remains the safer default interface for Findings, records, imports, and backups.

13. The ten golden rules

  1. Search before creating.
  2. One entity, one authoritative record.
  3. Keep IDs stable.
  4. Store facts in structured fields.
  5. Attach evidence directly.
  6. Use relationships instead of repeating information.
  7. Preserve uncertainty honestly.
  8. Review before publishing.
  9. Back up before replacing data.
  10. Treat the knowledge base as a living archival system, not a finished list.
